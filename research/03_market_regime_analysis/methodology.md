# Research 03: Market Regime Analysis — Methodology

## 1. Research Overview

This document describes the methodology used to investigate the relationship between cryptocurrency market regimes and algorithmic trading strategy performance.

The research evaluates how different strategy families behave under varying market conditions, including:

- Market trend conditions.
- Market direction.
- Market volatility.
- Combined market regimes.

The objective is to identify the market environments in which individual strategy families demonstrate stronger or weaker historical performance.

The research uses historical 30-minute market regime features and completed strategy trades generated by the AI Quantitative Trading Platform.

## 5. Market Regime Classification

Market conditions are evaluated across four dimensions.

### 5.1 Trend Regime Classification

The market is categorized into three states:

**TRENDING**

Represents market conditions exhibiting directional movement and sufficient trend strength.

Relevant features include:

- M30_Trend_Prob.
- M30_Trend_Strength.
- M30_Trend_Direction.
- ADX.
- Trend_Up.
- Trend_Down.
- HTF_Direction_Agreement.

**RANGING**

Represents market conditions exhibiting relatively limited directional movement.

Relevant features include:

- Is_Ranging_Regime.
- Sideways_Trend.
- Flat_EMA_Trend.
- ADX.
- Mean_Reversion_Potential.
- Range_Width_ATR.

**TRANSITION**

Represents market conditions where the market is changing between directional and non-directional states or where the existing trend structure is weakening.

Relevant features include:

- Is_Transition_Regime.
- M30_Trend_Prob_Delta.
- M30_Trend_Prob_Accel.
- M30_Reversal_Prob_Accel.
- HTF_Direction_Conflict.
- M30_Trend_Reversal_Spread.

The classification logic must ensure that every eligible market observation receives one mutually exclusive trend-state label.

Where the original regime flags overlap, an explicit classification precedence must be applied and documented in `analysis.py`.

The research must also report overlapping source flags separately during data validation so that the classification hierarchy does not hide inconsistencies.

### 5.2 Market Direction Classification

Market direction is categorized into:

- BULLISH
- BEARISH
- NEUTRAL, where applicable

Direction is determined using the available market trend-direction and directional-strength features.

The classification must be consistent across all strategy families.

Trade direction and market direction are treated as separate variables.

For example, a bullish market can contain both long and short trades. This allows the analysis to determine whether a strategy performs differently depending on the broader market direction.

### 5.3 Volatility Regime Classification

Market volatility is categorized into:

- LOW
- NORMAL
- HIGH

The classification uses volatility-related features such as:

- ATR_Ratio.
- ATR_Percentile.
- normalized_ATR.
- ATR_Expansion.
- BB_Width_Percentile.
- Volatility_Expansion.

Percentile-based classification is preferred because cryptocurrency pairs have different price scales and volatility characteristics.

The percentile thresholds must be calculated using historical information available at the observation timestamp.

For the proposed three-state classification:

| Volatility State | Percentile Range |
|---|---|
| LOW | At or below the 20th percentile |
| NORMAL | Between the 20th and 80th percentiles |
| HIGH | Above the 80th percentile |

Percentile calculations must be performed independently for each symbol using historical observations only.

A rolling or expanding historical reference window must be defined consistently in the implementation. Future observations must never influence historical volatility classifications.

### 5.4 Combined Market Regime

The combined regime represents the interaction of three dimensions:

1. Market direction.
2. Trend state.
3. Volatility state.

Examples:

- BULLISH / TRENDING / HIGH
- BULLISH / TRENDING / NORMAL
- BEARISH / TRENDING / HIGH
- BEARISH / TRANSITION / NORMAL
- BULLISH / RANGING / LOW
- BEARISH / RANGING / HIGH

This classification allows the research to investigate more specific market environments rather than evaluating trend, direction and volatility independently.

Neutral market observations should remain identifiable rather than being forced into bullish or bearish categories.

## 6. Timestamp Alignment and Trade Matching

Accurate timestamp alignment is essential to prevent look-ahead bias.

Each completed trade must be associated with the latest eligible market feature observation available at its entry decision time.

### 6.1 Market Feature Timestamp

The original market dataset contains a `Date` field representing the 30-minute candle timestamp.

The analysis requires a separate `MergeTime` field representing when the corresponding market features became available.

If `Date` represents the 30-minute candle opening time and the features are calculated using the completed candle, then:

```python
MergeTime = Date + pd.Timedelta(minutes=30)
```

This transformation must only be applied after verifying that `Date` represents candle-open time and that the feature calculations use the completed candle.

If the source timestamp already represents candle-close or feature-availability time, it must not be shifted again.

### 6.2 Trade Entry Matching

For each completed trade:

1. Identify the trading symbol.
2. Read the trade entry timestamp.
3. Locate market observations belonging to the same symbol.
4. Select the latest observation whose `MergeTime` is less than or equal to the trade entry decision timestamp.
5. Attach the selected market features to the trade.

The matching condition is:

```python
Market.MergeTime <= Trade.Entry_Time
```

The matching operation must also enforce symbol equality.

Conceptually:

```python
matched_trade = latest_market_observation(
    symbol=trade.Symbol,
    timestamp_condition=Market.MergeTime <= trade.Entry_Time
)
```

The implementation should use a backward as-of join or an equivalent symbol-aware matching procedure.

### 6.3 Feature Availability Validation

A successful timestamp match does not independently prove that the features were available for live execution.

The following must be verified:

- The 30-minute candle was fully closed.
- Indicators were calculated using completed candle data.
- H2 and H8 features were based on completed higher-timeframe candles.
- HTF predictions were generated using information available at that historical timestamp.
- No future candle information entered the feature calculations.
- The entry timestamp represents the actual decision or execution time consistently.

The analysis must record the time difference between the matched feature timestamp and the trade entry timestamp.

This is used to identify stale or incorrectly aligned observations.

### 6.4 Unmatched Trades

Trades without a valid preceding market observation must be excluded from regime-performance calculations.

The analysis must report:

- Total trades.
- Matched trades.
- Unmatched trades.
- Match rate.

Unmatched trades must not be silently discarded.

## 8. Performance Evaluation Metrics

Each strategy family is evaluated using the following metrics.

### 8.1 Total Trades

The number of completed trades within a particular market regime.

```python
Total_Trades = len(trades)
```

### 8.2 Winning Trades

The number of trades with positive net P&L.

```python
Winning_Trades = (Net_PnL > 0).sum()
```

### 8.3 Losing Trades

The number of trades with negative net P&L.

```python
Losing_Trades = (Net_PnL < 0).sum()
```

Zero-P&L trades should be reported separately where present.

### 8.4 Win Rate

The percentage of winning trades.

```python
Win_Rate = (
    Winning_Trades / Total_Trades
) * 100
```

### 8.5 Net P&L

The sum of net P&L across all trades in the group.

```python
Net_PnL = trades["Net_PnL"].sum()
```

The source trade P&L definition must be preserved. If it already includes trading fees and execution costs, those costs must not be deducted again.

### 8.6 Gross Profit

The sum of positive trade P&L.

```python
Gross_Profit = trades.loc[
    trades["Net_PnL"] > 0, "Net_PnL"
].sum()
```

### 8.7 Gross Loss

The absolute sum of negative trade P&L.

```python
Gross_Loss = abs(
    trades.loc[
        trades["Net_PnL"] < 0, "Net_PnL"
    ].sum()
)
```

### 8.8 Profit Factor

Profit Factor measures gross profits relative to gross losses.

```python
Profit_Factor = Gross_Profit / Gross_Loss
```

Interpretation:

| Profit Factor | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| Greater than 1.0 | Gross profits exceed gross losses |
| Equal to 1.0 | Gross profits equal gross losses |
| Less than 1.0 | Gross losses exceed gross profits |

If gross loss is zero, Profit Factor should be recorded as undefined or infinite according to the reporting convention, not arbitrarily set to zero.

### 8.9 Average P&L

```python
Average_PnL = Net_PnL / Total_Trades
```

All performance metrics must use consistent trade-level P&L definitions.

## 10. Statistical Reliability and Sample Size

Historical performance differences may be misleading when a regime contains very few trades.

Therefore, every regime comparison must include its trade count.

The analysis should distinguish:

- High-sample observations.
- Moderate-sample observations.
- Low-sample observations.

A suggested reporting convention is:

| Trade Count | Classification |
|---|---|
| Below 30 | Very low sample |
| 30–99 | Limited sample |
| 100–299 | Moderate sample |
| 300 or more | Larger sample |

These are reporting categories, not guarantees of statistical significance.

A high Profit Factor based on a small number of trades must not be treated as reliable evidence of strategy superiority.

Where appropriate, further validation can include:

- Confidence intervals for win rate.
- Bootstrap confidence intervals for average P&L.
- Monthly and yearly performance consistency.
- Out-of-sample validation.
- Walk-forward evaluation.

## 12. Bias Prevention and Research Limitations

The following safeguards are mandatory.

### 12.1 Look-Ahead Bias

Only information available at the historical trade decision timestamp may be used.

Future market observations, incomplete candles and future-generated predictions must not influence regime classification.

### 12.2 Survivorship and Universe Bias

The research uses the supplied 50-pair universe.

The findings should not automatically be generalized to all Binance Futures trading pairs or other exchanges.

### 12.3 Volatility Threshold Bias

Historical volatility thresholds must use only past information.

A global percentile calculated using the complete research period would introduce future information into earlier regime labels.

### 12.4 Sample Size Bias

Small trade samples can produce extreme performance metrics.

Trade counts must accompany every comparison.

### 12.5 Strategy Selection Bias

The research is descriptive.

Identifying a historically profitable strategy-regime combination does not prove that dynamically selecting strategies based on that combination will improve future performance.

Any regime-based selection or allocation rules must be validated separately using out-of-sample and walk-forward testing.
